# B. Timber legality (highest-consequence area)

Prepared 2026-09-25. Tags per index.

## B1. CITES - Dalbergia (sheesham / Indian rosewood)
- WHAT: The genus Dalbergia (except Appendix I species) is on CITES Appendix II with Annotation #15 (valid since 2023-02-23). Dalbergia sissoo (sheesham) is a Dalbergia species. Annotation #15 covers logs, sawn wood, veneer, plywood and, subject to exemptions, other parts and derivatives. Source: https://cites.org/eng/app/appendices.php and https://www.aphis.usda.gov/sites/default/files/cites.pdf (VERIFIED that the genus/annotation exists; not verified whether D. sissoo has a species-level annotation that differs).
- EXEMPTION (10 kg): Annotation #15 has an exemption for small weights. Sources read give two different wordings: "finished products, up to 10 kg of Dalbergia wood per shipment" vs "non-commercial exports of max total weight 10 kg per shipment". CITES CoP20 Doc 104 annex says weight is assessed per annotated species per item, not on total shipment weight (VERIFIED via search snippet, https://cites.org/sites/default/files/documents/E-CoP20-104.pdf). We could NOT confirm the exact current legal text nor whether CoP20 (Samarkand, 2025-11-24 to 12-05) changed it - the report describing exemption (c) called it "sound" (VERIFIED snippet). TREAT AS UNVERIFIED: do not rely on any 10 kg rule for commercial B2C parcels until CITES Management Authority (India) or EPCH gives written confirmation. Commercial sale = likely NOT "non-commercial" (ASSUMPTION).
- Practical safe route: (a) get an India CITES export permit per consignment for sheesham items, or (b) switch to non-CITES species for US-bound goods (mango, acacia, teak from plantation, neem, etc.), (c) declare species truthfully either way. US Fish and Wildlife Service import rules also apply to CITES wood (Form 3-177 declaration, designated port, FWS inspection) - confirm with broker [BROKER] (ASSUMPTION).
- WHO: Owner + EPCH; CITES Management Authority India (MoEFCC/DGFT process); WHEN: before first sheesham shipment; COST: permit fee low (Rs few hundred) plus lead time 1-4 weeks (ESTIMATE); Also Vriksh certificate (EPCH scheme for legally-sourced timber for handicrafts, used to prove non-forest/farm origin - ASSUMPTION, verify scheme name and current issuer with EPCH).

## B2. Lacey Act declaration (US)
- WHAT: Importer must file a declaration for plant products, incl. wooden furniture and articles (Phase VII from 2024-12-01 covers remaining plant HTS codes not 100% composite; some furniture 9403.40.90.xx codes added later). Data: importer, entry number, product HTS, description, quantity and unit, value, scientific name (genus/species) of each plant, country of harvest (VERIFIED, https://www.aphis.usda.gov/plant-imports/file-lacey-act-declaration ). Filing via CBP ACE or APHIS LAWGS; paper PPQ 505 discontinued from 2026-01-01 (VERIFIED). De minimis disclaimer Code G for plant weight <=5% of unit and <=2.9 kg per line (VERIFIED) - not useful for solid-wood furniture.
- WHO: Importer of Record/customs broker files; the seller supplies species and harvest data. WHEN: every entry. COST: broker Rs/USD 10-25 per line (ESTIMATE). If you cannot state the species, use "special use" only where APHIS allows; otherwise you cannot lawfully file.
- Penalties: forfeiture of goods, fines, and criminal liability for false declarations; due care standard (ASSUMPTION - 16 USC 3372/3373; check with attorney) [LAWYER].
- Due care: know the species, origin, supplier, keep records to show reasonable inquiry.

## B3. Proof of legal origin (what Jodhpur workshops should keep)
- Timber purchase invoices (with GSTIN, species, quantity), transit permit/pass for movement of forest produce where required by state (Rajasthan Forest rules) - verify with Rajasthan Forest Dept; farm-grown timber (sheesham grown on private land) is generally exempt from felling permits in Rajasthan but transit pass may be needed (ASSUMPTION - confirm).
- Sawmill/depot licence copy, supplier declaration of species and origin, photographs of stock, batch/lot code mapping timber to SKUs, EPCH Vriksh certificate where issued. Keep 5+ years (align with CBP recordkeeping).
- Never buy wood from unknown "loose" traders; avoid imported wood without import papers (a Lacey import trail issue).

## B4. ISPM-15 and pests
- WHAT: Wood packaging material (pallets, crates, dunnage) must be heat-treated (HT) or methyl-bromide fumigated (MB) and stamped with IPPC mark by an NPPO-authorised provider (India: NPPO under Ministry of Agriculture; agencies accredited). Applies to solid wood packaging; plywood/OSB/paper and cardboard are exempt. USDA APHIS enforces: https://www.aphis.usda.gov/plant-imports/wood-packaging (ASSUMPTION - not fetched). Non-compliant crates can be re-exported/destroyed.
- Finished furniture itself is not covered by ISPM-15, but APHIS can inspect for bark, insects; deliver clean, kiln-dried timber. Prefer cardboard/plywood packing, corner protectors in card, no loose solid wood.
- WHO: exporter/packer; WHEN: every shipment; COST: Rs 1k-5k per consignment for treated crates (ESTIMATE).

## B5. Naming
- Use the botanical name in invoices and Lacey filings: "Dalbergia sissoo (sheesham)". Marketing may say "sheesham (Indian rosewood)". Do NOT say just "rosewood": FTC and species-mislabel risk and consumer confusion with Dalbergia nigra/latifolia (Appendix I/II commercial rosewoods) (FTC Guides on deception; ASSUMPTION). Say "mango wood" (Mangifera indica), "acacia" (Acacia nilotica/ Vachellia), "teak" (Tectona grandis), "sal" (Shorea robusta).
- Add species and origin to the product page and packing list; be consistent with invoices.
